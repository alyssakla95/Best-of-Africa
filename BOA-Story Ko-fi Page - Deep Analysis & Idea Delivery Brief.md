## 1. What BOA-Story Actually Is

BOA-Story is a **living digital platform** built to surface real, grounded stories about African lives, cities, creators, and everyday opportunity — explicitly positioned *against* the dominant media framing of Africa as a place of crisis, charity, and disaster.

The creator defines it directly:

> *"A digital home for real, thoughtful stories about African lives, cities, and ideas — beyond charity ads and disaster headlines."*

It is not a news outlet, not a charity, and not a personal blog. It is a **narrative correction platform** — a media and editorial product being built in public, iteratively, by a student and independent writer.

The platform is currently in **prototype and pre-launch stage**, and the Ko-fi page is the primary mechanism for converting early believers into financial backers who make the full launch possible.

---

## 2. The Founder's Voice and Personal Framing

Mailles Cortes presents himself as a **student and independent writer** — not a CEO, not a media executive, not an NGO representative. This is a deliberate and strategically important positioning choice.

The About section reads:

> *"I'm a student and independent writer trying to close the gap between the Africa you see in headlines and the Africa I hear about from friends, founders, and family. Here I'm building BOA-Story, a small, self-funded project to surface grounded stories about African cities, creators, and everyday opportunity."*

This framing does several things at once:
- Establishes **authenticity** — this is personal, not institutional
- Signals **insider perspective** — the Africa described is the one lived and heard, not consumed from foreign media
- Communicates **resource constraint honestly** — "small, self-funded" doesn't ask for sympathy; it contextualizes why the Ko-fi funding matters
- Creates **relatability** — especially for diaspora audiences who recognize the exact gap being described
