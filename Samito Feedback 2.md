“Thank you for sharing the materials and the current status of Best of Africa following your conversation with Mailles.

Having reviewed everything carefully, one thing is very clear: the technical foundation of the platform is solid, ambitious, and well above average — both in terms of integrations and the sophistication of the information aggregation engine.

After taking a step back, I have been thinking about Best of Africa not only as a technology or content platform, but as a product that needs to generate predictable revenue from the outset, without compromising its longer-term vision.
Below, I am sharing a structured strategic view of the complete customer journey — from first contact to subscription — with a very explicit focus on clarity, focus and monetisation.”

1. Foundational decision: focus on a premium niche

To ensure monetisation from the start, Best of Africa should, at this stage, focus on a clearly defined, high–purchasing-power niche: investors, DFIs, governments, corporations and entrepreneurs making strategic decisions related to Africa.

This focus does not limit the product — it enables it.
It creates clarity of message, a decisive user journey, and faster validation of paid demand.

1. User mindset before the first click

The user does not arrive out of curiosity.
They arrive because they have a decision to make, limited time, and are overwhelmed by fragmented information.

What they are looking for is not narrative, but:
• reduced uncertainty,
• actionable clarity,
• and informational advantage.

1. First contact (first seconds)

The first contact will rarely be the Home page.
In most cases, it will be a briefing, an alert, or a sector or country insight.

Above the fold, the user must immediately understand:
• who the platform is for,
• what problem it solves,
• and why it is different.

Clear signals of timeliness (date, time, sector, country) are essential to establish immediate credibility.

1. First micro-win (first minutes)

In the first few minutes, the user must gain something tangible:
• a better framing,
• a clearer synthesis,
• an insight that saves time and cuts through noise.

At this stage, it is not about full depth, but about superior clarity.

At around 60% of the content, a light and elegant friction point should appear, making it clear that full access is part of the subscription. The goal is to trigger the right question:

“What is the cost of not having this on a recurring basis?”

1. Guided exploration (value confirmation, not distraction)

If the user continues exploring, the platform should confirm value rather than create noise.

Navigation should be short and intentional, showing only:
• related intelligence,
• recent alerts,
• covered sectors and countries,

while consistently reinforcing that this level of access is reserved for subscribers.

1. Decision moment

The subscription page must be absolutely clear and concrete.

Essential elements:
• Who the product is for (investors, governments, corporations and entrepreneurs).
• What the user receives, in practical and verifiable terms:
• regular briefings,
• market and policy alerts,
• sector and country intelligence,
• premium reports.
• A simple price that is easy to decide.
• Immediate access and flexible cancellation.

At this stage, simplicity is a competitive advantage.

1. Payment

Payment should be:
• direct,
• simple,
• without calls,
• without administrative friction.

Every additional step here reduces conversion.

1. Immediate value confirmation

Immediately after payment, the user should feel:

“This was already worth it.”

That happens if they have instant access to:
• the day’s briefing,
• relevant alerts,
• their selected sectors and countries.

This moment is decisive for retention.

1. Habit formation and recurring revenue

With a predictable and consistent rhythm, the product becomes part of the user’s decision-making process.
This is where recurring revenue is built — value is perceived continuously, not occasionally.
