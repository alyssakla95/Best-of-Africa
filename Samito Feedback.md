## Optimal MVP sectors maximize investment momentum against information gaps

Analysis of FDI flows, government priorities, intelligence coverage, and business infrastructure across the four priority countries yields twelve recommended sector focuses—three per country—with clear prioritization for launch sequence.

### Mozambique: LNG, critical minerals, and renewable energy

Natural Gas/LNG emerges as the highest-priority sector with over $50 billion in committed and planned investments. TotalEnergies' $20 billion Mozambique LNG project lifted force majeure in October 2025 after the Cabo Delgado security situation stabilized sufficiently, with construction resuming and first gas targeted for 2029–2030. Eni's Coral South FLNG has been operational since November 2022, producing 3.4 million tonnes annually. The $25 billion Rovuma LNG project (ExxonMobil 40%, Eni 40%) expects FID in H1 2026. Information gaps are acute—contractor/supplier intelligence, local content compliance tracking, and community development monitoring are poorly served by existing providers.

Mining and Critical Minerals represents the second priority, driven by Mozambique's position as the world's largest graphite producer (25 million tonnes in reserves, supplying 10% of global demand). The Montepuez ruby operation produced 3.9 million carats in 2024, up 46% year-over-year. The U.S. DFC provided a $150 million loan for the Balama graphite mine as part of a China-diversification strategy for EV battery supply chains. License tracking, ESG compliance monitoring, and artisanal sector documentation remain fragmented.

Renewable Energy completes the portfolio with the $5 billion Mphanda Nkuwa hydropower project (1,500 MW), backed by World Bank/EDF/TotalEnergies and targeting 2031 operations. The government's Energy Transition Strategy targets $80 billion in investment through 2050, with installed capacity expected to grow from 2,900 MW to 9,472 MW by 2030.

### Angola: Mining, agriculture, and energy transformation

Mining and Critical Minerals leads Angola's opportunity matrix. Diamond production doubled from 7 million to 14 million carats between 2017–2024, generating $1.49 billion in revenue with $2.1 billion projected for 2025. De Beers returned to Angola in 2024, discovering 8 new kimberlite targets with ENDIAMA. The $6+ billion Lobito Corridor multilateral investment transforms mineral transport capabilities, with the DFC providing $553 million for railway rehabilitation increasing capacity from 0.4 to 4.6 million metric tons annually.

Agriculture and Agribusiness represents the second priority, with PRODESI approving over 1,020 projects worth 732.3 billion kwanzas—agriculture leads with 557 projects (55% of total). The market is expected to reach $8.51 billion in 2025, growing to $11.7 billion by 2030. Coffee production targets a return to historical levels of 240,000 tonnes from the current 7,584 tonnes, with exports up 51% in 2024. Information gaps are severe—there is limited reliable data on agricultural land availability, crop yields, and supply chains in English.

Renewable Energy shows strong momentum with the $1.6 billion U.S. EXIM loan to Sun Africa for solar mini-grids—the largest U.S. renewables investment in Africa. The 2.17 GW Caculo Cabaça hydropower plant (Angola's largest) commissioned its first phase in 2024. Only 43% of the population has electricity access, creating substantial market opportunity.

### Namibia: Green hydrogen, oil and gas, and uranium

Green Hydrogen is the standout opportunity with $10+ billion committed through the Hyphen project alone. ENERTRAG/Nicholas Holdings targets 350,000 tonnes of hydrogen and 2 million tonnes of ammonia annually by 2029. The Namibian government holds a 24% equity stake via the SDG Namibia One Fund. Germany designated Hyphen as a “strategic foreign project” in March 2024, and the EU committed €50 million for green hydrogen development. Production costs of $1.73–$2.30/kg represent global competitiveness. Information gaps are high—project-level data remains scattered across developer sites, and commercial intelligence on offtake negotiations with Germany, the EU, and Japan is limited.

Oil and Gas has transformed Namibia's prospects, with Orange Basin discoveries potentially containing 15+ billion barrels equivalent. Galp's Mopane discovery estimates 10 billion barrels; TotalEnergies' Venus-1X holds 5.1 billion barrels with FID expected 2025–2026 and first oil targeted for 2029. The exploration success rate of 75–80% (9 of 11–12 wells declaring discoveries) is unprecedented globally. Chevron, QatarEnergy, and ExxonMobil have all acquired acreage.

Mining maintains its traditional importance, with Namibia ranking as the world's third-largest uranium producer (11% of global production). Five new uranium mines are expected within five years as spot prices hit $106/lb in January 2024—a 16-year high. The June 2023 export ban on unprocessed lithium, cobalt, manganese, graphite, and rare earths signals a value-addition focus.

### Tanzania: Critical minerals, LNG, and luxury tourism

Mining and Critical Minerals leads Tanzania's opportunity profile with sector GDP contribution increasing from 7.2% to 10.1% between 2021–2024, exceeding government targets. Total mining investment reached $10.95 billion across 915 projects. The Kabanga nickel project (Lifezone Metals) raised $75 million in H2 2025—it represents one of the world's largest undeveloped nickel sulfide deposits. Barrick Gold invested $558 million in H1 2025. The 2025 government report identifies 14 value-addition opportunities across 11 minerals worth $7.2–11.7 billion annually.

Natural Gas/LNG represents a $30–42 billion mega-project with 57 trillion cubic feet in proven offshore reserves. Shell (Blocks 1 & 4) and Equinor (Block 2) have concluded framework agreements, though FID has been delayed to 2028 with first LNG now expected in 2032. Information gaps are very high—negotiations remain opaque with limited public disclosure of terms.

Tourism showed record performance with 5.36 million total visitors in 2024 (beating the 2025 target early), generating $3.9 billion in revenue. Four Seasons is developing a new 60-villa resort in Zanzibar. Chinese visitors recorded the highest spend at $491.8 per person per night. The Standard Gauge Railway now connects Dar es Salaam to key regions, reducing travel times dramatically.
